All RNGdle Badges — Every Badge, EP Value and Family

233 badges, 39 scoring families, and the one rule that decides which ones actually count.

RNGdle has 233 badges, sorted into 39 scoring families. Each badge carries an EP value, and a number's EP is the sum of the badges it triggers — with one rule that changes everything: within any single family, only the highest-scoring badge counts. This page lists all 233 RNGdle badges with their EP values, how each is earned, and its family.

How Many RNGdle Badges Are There?

There are 233 badges in RNGdle, organised into 39 scoring families. A family is a group of badges that describe the same property of a number — so a badge family is not a difficulty band, and it is not a rarity tier. It is simply the bucket that tells the scoring engine which badges compete with each other.
What 	Count 	What it means
Badges 	233 	Every badge a number can trigger
Scoring families 	39 	Groups that compete internally for EP
Badges counted per family 	1 	Only the highest-scoring badge survives

A further 65 badges carry no family in the official table. They still compete with each other under an empty bucket, so the de-duplication rule applies to them too. That leaves 40 family buckets in total.

That last row is the whole ballgame. 233 badges, but a single number never banks anywhere near 233 of them toward its score.
The Rule That Decides Which Badges Count

Every badge has an EP value. Trigger several badges and your number's EP goes up — but not by the sum of everything you triggered.

The family de-duplication rule: when a number triggers more than one badge inside the same family, only the highest-scoring badge in that family is added to the score. The rest are discarded, no matter how valuable they would have been on their own.

Two consequences follow, and both are visible across the entire RNGdle score range:

1. Score totals come from a small, closed set. Because the badge table is fixed and each family contributes at most one badge, the number of possible EP totals is far smaller than 233. Most numbers collapse onto a handful of totals — which is why 83 numbers tie at the 1,759 EP floor and 2,119 numbers tie at 3,337 EP, the most common score in the game.

2. The top of the scale is nearly empty. Reaching the maximum, 186,186,584 EP, means taking the highest badge in every one of the 39 families at once. Exactly one number out of all 1,000,001 manages it: number 1.

The rule is not just a line in the docs. Of the 233 badges, 3 are never counted for any number — a higher-EP badge in the same family always wins, so they unlock but never add score.
